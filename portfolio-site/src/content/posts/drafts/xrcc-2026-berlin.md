Berlin, XRCC 2026, what a week.

I came to XRCC as a finalist, excited to build, learn, and meet people working at the edge of XR, AI, spatial computing and wearable technology.

I left with new friends, new ideas, better questions, and a much stronger belief in where this space is heading.

XRCC was more than a hackathon or a conference. It was a room full of people who genuinely cared about building the future of immersive technology and helping each other get there.

---

## From an online qualifier to the finals in Berlin

Our journey to XRCC started before any of us arrived in Germany.

The online qualifier gave our team the chance to develop the idea behind **InsureVision AI**, an XR and AI-powered insurance inspection experience designed to help inspectors capture evidence, document damage and produce clearer information from the field.

Our team was spread across different countries and time zones. We built, tested, pitched, debugged and refined the application remotely, often working around one another's schedules to keep things moving.

Making it through as a finalist meant we could finally bring that work into the same room.

To my teammates, **Adquena Faine, Falguni, M.Sadain and Benilda**, thank you. I am proud of what we created, but I am even prouder of how we worked together. After months of calls and messages, it was so good to finally meet face to face.

Our honorary team member, **Daniel Oquelis**, also deserves a huge thank you. His mentorship and support helped us move from early ideation toward something that felt like a polished product rather than just a hackathon concept.

A special shoutout has to go to **Adquena**. Watching her push beyond her comfort zone, step onto the stage and own the pitch was one of my favourite parts of the entire event. She absolutely belonged in that room.

## Learning from people who helped shape my XR journey

One of the most meaningful parts of XRCC was reconnecting with **Dilmer Valecillos** and **Roberto Coviello**.

Both have played an important part in my journey into XR. Their knowledge, encouragement and willingness to share what they have learned helped me when I was still finding my feet in this space.

Seeing them again in Berlin made me feel like I am on the right track.

There is something powerful about meeting people who first influenced you through tutorials, online communities or earlier events, then being able to stand beside them years later as a fellow builder. It is a reminder that progress often happens through small steps, shared knowledge and people who take the time to help others grow.

XRCC gave me several more of those moments.

**Oscar Falmer** took the time to help me better understand the development pathway for Meta Ray-Ban Displays, including the strengths and limitations of both DAT and web app approaches. That practical guidance helped me make sense of where each option fits and how developers should think about building for emerging wearable platforms.

**Alisa Kurt** explained the power of the IWSDK and the possibilities it opens for mixed reality development. Those conversations connected a lot of dots for me and gave me a clearer picture of how spatial experiences can be built around more natural interactions.

**Robert Alexander Heinrich** spent time testing our application, giving us detailed feedback and explaining the broader vision he is working toward in the XR space. That kind of direct, informed feedback is incredibly valuable when you are trying to move an idea beyond a prototype.

**Daniel Sproll** also shared his insights on sound design in VR. Sound can easily become an afterthought during a fast build, but it plays a huge role in presence, attention and how comfortable an experience feels. His perspective gave me a lot to think about for future projects.

## Access to technology I cannot normally develop with in Australia

One of the biggest highlights was getting hands-on access to technology that I cannot currently develop with at home in Australia.

That included the **Meta Ray-Ban Displays** and **Snap Spectacles**.

There is a major difference between reading documentation or watching a product demo and actually having the device in your hands. Once you can test the interaction model, understand the limitations and see how people naturally use the hardware, the ideas become much more grounded.

It also changes the questions you ask.

Instead of wondering what might be possible in theory, you start thinking about what is useful, what feels natural, where the friction is, and what an experience needs to do to justify living on that device.

Being able to test, build and think through real use cases on the hardware was a massive opportunity. It was also a reminder of how uneven access to emerging technology can be depending on where you live.

For developers in Australia, it can be difficult to experiment early with new platforms when the devices, SDKs or development programs are not yet available locally. Events like XRCC help close that gap by putting the technology, experts and builders in the same place.

## A competition that never really felt competitive

One of the best parts of XRCC was how collaborative it felt.

We were technically competing, but it never felt like people were holding back or hoping someone else would fail.

Teams tested one another's demos, shared feedback, helped debug problems, asked better questions and genuinely wanted each other to succeed. In several cases, people working on similar ideas found that their projects solved different parts of the same larger problem.

That atmosphere made the event feel different from a traditional hackathon.

The goal was not only to win. It was to build something meaningful, learn as much as possible and leave with stronger ideas than the ones you arrived with.

A big thank you to the builders and new friends I crossed paths with, including **Sushant Srikrish, Finley Yuheng Sun, Frédéric Franken, Pratik Mane, Jia-Yin Shiu, Ronit Saha and Shreya Bansal**, along with many others I met throughout the week.

The conversations, demos and late-night build energy, fuelled by plenty of **Club-Mate**, were a massive highlight.

## Why being in the room matters

XRCC reminded me why being in the room still matters.

You can learn an incredible amount online. Remote collaboration allowed our team to build InsureVision AI across countries and time zones, and online communities have played a huge part in my own XR journey.

But there is something different about standing beside people who are building the future in real time.

A quick conversation at a booth can change the direction of a feature. A mentor can explain something in ten minutes that would have taken hours to piece together alone. Testing another team's prototype can trigger an idea you would never have reached from behind your own screen.

The technology was exciting, but the people made the experience valuable.

XR is just as much about people as it is about headsets, hardware or demos.

It is about people building.

People testing.

People believing.

People pushing each other further.
